The Investor Problem

Markets produce information faster than anyone can read it

Price movements, company announcements, earnings, macro data, analyst revisions, news, sector rotations, market-wide shocks, and the web of relationships between companies — all of it arrives continuously, and almost none of it matters to any particular investor on any particular day.

The failure mode is concrete. A single holding can generate ten or fifteen alert emails a day from brokers and news services — and none of them get read, because reading them all is a job. The information exists; the evaluation doesn't. News never answers the questions that actually decide whether to look up from your day:

What the noise asks of you

  • Is this important, or routine?
  • Is the price move meaningful, or normal volatility?
  • Does it affect anything I hold?
  • What else could it affect?
  • Do I need to investigate — right now?

What most investors do instead

  • Delete the alerts unread
  • Skim headlines and hope tone equals importance
  • Check prices, which show direction but not meaning
  • Find out about the thing that mattered a week late

Oreum exists to answer six questions, continuously and with evidence:

1 What changed?
2 How significant is it?
3 What does it affect?
4 Does it affect my portfolio?
5 How much attention does it deserve?
6 Why?

The investor may hold stocks, ETFs, or both, potentially across multiple brokers. Oreum's job is not to bring them more information — it is to absorb the flood and return one or two moments of genuine signal a day.

What Oreum Is

A suppression engine with an investigation surface behind it

Oreum is a data-driven market-intelligence tool for investors who want to understand what is happening without constantly researching it themselves. Its primary output is restraint: one or two emails a day, sent only when something crosses an attention threshold — and silence when nothing does.

The product inverts the alert-email ratio. Instead of fifteen unread notifications per holding, the investor receives a single daily filter that takes one or two minutes to read, and may correctly say:

“Nothing significant requires your attention today.” A quiet day is a successful result — and it is only credible because the email shows what was checked and suppressed.

When something does matter, the email names it, gives its direction and why, and links into the platform — the Feed for what happened across the market, the Ticker for investigating a single company in depth. The email drives every platform visit; the platform earns the trust the email spends.

The differentiator: second-order intelligence

Oreum operates over a small, densely connected, hand-curated universe of companies in which one company's capital expenditure is another company's revenue. Direct news about a holding, the investor will see anywhere. The connection they would have missed — a customer raising capex, a foundry constraint, a competitor's guidance — is the product.

The most important information may not mention your ticker. News about Microsoft's data-center spending is news about Broadcom's order book — whether or not any headline says so. Oreum's context graph exists to surface exactly this class of information, clearly labelled as inference, never disguised as fact.

Product Principles

Five commitments the system is built around

Know what changed. Know why it matters. Ignore the rest. The product's value is measured by what it leaves out. Every suppressed non-event is the work.
Optimize for attention efficiency, not engagement. Oreum has no incentive to be opened often. It has every incentive to be trusted when it speaks.
A quiet day is a successful result. This is enforced structurally: attention thresholds are absolute, never rank-normalized — so a day where nothing crosses them produces nothing. A system that always has a “top story” cannot be quiet.
Math decides. The LLM explains. Every decision that ranks, scores, or fires is deterministic, reproducible, and backtestable. Language models read messy text and write clear prose; they hold no authority over attention.
The most important information may not mention your ticker. Second-order propagation through observed relationships is the differentiator — and it is always presented as inference, with its path visible.

Two supporting invariants run through everything: facts are preserved before intelligence is derived — actual, expected, previous, and reference values are stored before any meaning is computed from them — and observed facts, known events, and inferred relationships remain visually and structurally distinct at every layer, from database to email.

The Oreum Intelligence Model

Ten concepts that refuse to collapse into each other

Most market products fail by conflation: the news story becomes the signal becomes the alert. Oreum's vocabulary keeps each question separate, because each needs different evidence and different validation.

Fact
A normalized representation of source information, preserved before any meaning is derived. For measurable events: actual, expected, previous, reference.
Observation
A measured or calculated property of market data. Daily return, volume ratio, sector-relative return, volatility-adjusted return.
Event
A structured representation of something that happened. Earnings release, guidance change, Fed decision, analyst revision, CPI surprise.
Signal
An analytical conclusion derived from events and observations. Unusual price move, unusual volume, earnings surprise, unusual relative performance.
Context
The surrounding market, sector, macro, and relationship information needed to interpret a signal. Cross-cutting input, not a computation stage.
Impact
What another entity could be affected by, and through which relationship. Impact is possibility, not importance.
Relevance
How much a signal or impact touches a particular investor's watchlist or portfolio. The only per-user layer in the pipeline.
Attention
How much this investor should care about the situation right now. Deterministic, experimental until validated, displayed 0–10.
Intelligence Item
The user-facing aggregation of related signals, events, impacts, context, evidence, and attention — one situation, not five alerts.
Explanation
A concise human-readable account of why the item matters, backed by source evidence, with inference clearly marked.

Event ≠ Signal ≠ Impact ≠ Relevance ≠ Attention. And direction ≠ attention: a −2% day can deserve a 3, a −0.8% day can deserve an 8. Collapsing any of these boundaries reproduces a product that already exists — a news feed, a movers list, or a black-box score.

Context & Second-Order Effects

The graph discovers impact. It never decides attention.

Context is interconnected rather than strictly hierarchical — macro conditions shape markets, sectors, companies, and portfolios, and effects run in both directions.

Macro↔ Market↔ Sector↔ Company↔ Portfolio

Companies are connected by typed, hand-curated relationships — supplier · customer · competitor · sector · technology · geography · macro · market. The graph answers one question: who could this affect, and through what relationship? It proposes propagation paths; it holds no vote on how much anything matters.

A second-order example

Observed: Microsoft raises capital-expenditure guidance, citing AI infrastructure demand.

MSFT capex guidance ↑→ customer relationship→ possible order-book tailwind: AVGO, NVDA, ANET

One observed event, one observed edge, one clearly-labelled inference. The investor holding Broadcom sees why this reached them — the path is part of the item.

A macro chain — and why Oreum is careful with it:

Oil +8%→ inflation pressure→ rates expectations→ technology pressure→ possible impact: NVDA / AVGO

A user does not need to own oil for this to matter — but every hop here is an inference, and chained inferences degrade fast. Propagation is therefore constrained: one hop in the initial system, a second hop only across two observed relationships, and macro chains presented as context rather than computed impact until they can carry confidence honestly.

The system distinguishes, at every surface: observed facts, known events, and inferred relationships. Inference is rendered with lighter visual weight and named as inference. Nothing inferred is ever presented as certain.

The Attention Engine

How much should this investor care, right now?

Attention is the product's central number — and its most disciplined one. It is deterministic, absolute, historically validated, and separate from direction.

NVDA −2.0% 3/10

Ordinary volatility for this name. No event, volume unremarkable, in line with the sector. Suppressed.

NVDA −0.8% 8/10

Small move, material unexpected event, unusual volume. Fires — the move is not the story.

Attention renders on a neutral scale — never green or red. Color belongs to direction alone.

Version 1 — decided structure, undecided weights

Observations

  • 1-day return · rolling volatility · volatility-adjusted return
  • market & sector return · market- & sector-relative return
  • volume, and volume vs. rolling baseline
  • later: volatility expansion, multi-day movement

Signal families

  • price anomaly
  • relative-performance anomaly
  • volume anomaly

Conceptual composition

signal strength
+ context
+ impact
+ personal relevance
+ evidence / confidence
────────────────
→ attention

Exact weights are deliberately not fixed — v1 is an experiment, and no weighting is presented as final truth before historical validation.

Two structural decisions that are fixed

  • Thresholds are absolute, never rank-normalized. Scoring against the day's cross-section always crowns a “top mover,” making quiet days impossible. Attention compares a situation to its own history.
  • Fire-or-silent precedes 0–10. The first engine is a threshold deciding whether the email says anything at all; the graded score grows out of it when the Feed needs ranking.

RSI and moving averages are ticker context, not attention inputs. RSI 72 does not mean sell.

Validation & Backtesting

“Better than the biggest movers” — proven, not asserted

The goal is not price prediction. The goal is: can Oreum identify meaningful situations while suppressing normal market noise? That claim is testable, and the system is built to be tested.

Baselines it must beat

B1BaselineWhat it represents
B1Biggest percentage moversWhat every finance app already shows
B2Volatility-adjusted moversMovers, corrected for each name's normal behavior
B3Move + volumeMovement with participation behind it
B4Move + relative performance + volumeThe strongest simple deterministic screen

Ground truth is constructed, not assumed

Attention has no natural label, so the target is built from two sources: weak labels — known event dates (earnings, guidance changes, major announcements), checkable in retrospect, giving recall targets for free — and human labels — retrospective judgment of “would I have wanted to be told about this that day?”, recorded with timestamp and basis so they stay point-in-time honest.

What success measures

  • Recall of major events at high attention — a missed earnings blowup is the catastrophic failure
  • Precision at the top band — items that fire should be endorsed in hindsight
  • Quiet-day rate vs. baselines — a system that is never quiet has failed its own definition of success
  • Stability — small input perturbations must not flip scores across bands, across market regimes

What backtesting must avoid

  • Look-ahead bias and future-information leakage — replay uses only what was knowable that day
  • Treating revised or restated information as if it was historically known — adjusted price series restate on splits and dividends, and derived data carries a recompute-on-restatement policy
  • Survivorship bias, acknowledged where the curated universe implies it
  • Optimizing prediction by accident: future returns may serve as supporting evidence — high-attention items being followed by elevated realized volatility is a sanity check — but never as the objective. A system optimized on future returns is a price predictor with a different name.

Math Decides. LLM Explains.

An authority boundary, not a pipeline position

Language models participate early — turning messy text into structure — but their output there is untrusted input, schema-validated like any external payload. Only prose is ever the LLM's final word. Nothing a model says can move an attention score.

Deterministic systems own

  • calculations and normalization
  • anomaly detection and comparisons
  • signal strength
  • relevance
  • deterministic impact computation
  • attention and ranking
  • objectively measurable direction
  • historical replay and backtesting

The LLM owns

  • reading messy, unstructured information
  • extraction and classification
  • entity identification
  • evidence extraction
  • summarization
  • explanation

…and is never the authority for the attention score.

Why the boundary holds

Trust in a filtering product dies from confident wrongness. Two rules keep the model honest by construction:

  • Impact direction derives from the relationship and event type, never from headline sentiment. A customer's capex increase is mechanically a supplier tailwind; how the headline sounds is noise. A wrong direction costs trust that does not come back.
  • Every explanation retains evidence and source attribution. Claims sit next to their sources; inference is marked as inference. Source facts and links are stored — never full article text.

At the current universe size, entity identification is an alias table and string matching, not a model capability — the LLM is reserved for problems that actually need one.

Product Surfaces

Email leads. Feed and Ticker investigate.

Email — “Do I need to care?”

The primary surface. One to two minutes, once or twice a day. It fires on threshold-crossing situations, stays silent otherwise, and always shows its work:

Oreum — Daily intelligenceTue 1 Sep · 1 min read
NVDA +10.2% New China supply agreement announced; volume 4.1× baseline. 9
INTC −11.4% CEO steps down; largest single-day move in 14 months. 8
25 companies checked · 47 stories evaluated · 45 suppressed — everything else was noise.

The filtered-out counter is permanent — a quiet email is only credible when the product shows what it suppressed.

Feed — “What happened?”

Attention-ranked Intelligence Items in two views — My Stocks and Broader Market. Each card carries what happened, direction, attention, concise explanation, context, and evidence. Direction and attention never share a visual channel.

Ticker — “Why is it happening?”

The investigation surface: current price, chart and historical trajectory, volume, relative performance, events and news, market/sector context, exposures, connections and indirect effects — with explanation and evidence. RSI and moving averages appear as context, never as advice.

Live Market Intelligence

Four clocks, honestly labelled

Oreum is not a once-per-day price application. When the investigation surface is open during market hours, the price must move — a platform showing $190 at 2pm because the daily bar was fetched at 9:30 is lying by omission. Different systems run on different clocks, and each shows its timestamp.

Live / Near-liveCurrent price, near-live volume, market status, provider timestampprovider decision open
IntradayObservations, anomalies, signals — attention updates deliberately deferredplanned
Event-drivenEarnings, news, macro events, material company announcementsplanned
DailyFinalized bars, daily intelligence, the emailoperational

Live market state and finalized historical bars are intentionally separate concepts, and they never impersonate each other: historical closes are labelled with their trade date, live prices with their provider timestamp and freshness state.

Sequencing decision: the v1 daily email needs no live provider at all — it works entirely from finalized bars the pipeline already holds. The live layer is its own roadmap phase, gated on an explicit provider decision: acceptable delay (true real-time vs. 15-minute delayed), cost, and rate limits, recorded before integration rather than discovered after. This ordering keeps the primary surface unblocked while the platform earns its live upgrade.

The current provider integration supplies historical daily data only — a provider limitation, not the product architecture. The historical layer's freshness is already monitored end-to-end (cache age reported by the health endpoint, staleness thresholds tuned so weekends don't cry wolf).

Example Situations

The model, walked through four mornings

The move that isn't a story

AMD −2.3% on an ordinary session — volatility in line with its history, volume at baseline, the sector down similarly, no event. Every observation is computed; no signal crosses its threshold. Nothing fires. This non-email is the product working.

The story that isn't a move

NVDA −0.8%, but an unexpected material event landed and volume is far above baseline. Price anomaly: none. Event signal plus volume anomaly: strong. Attention 8 — the email explains the event, not the price.

The second-order morning

Microsoft raises capex guidance. The investor holds Broadcom, not Microsoft. The event maps through an observed customer relationship to a possible order-book tailwind, direction derived from the edge type — mechanically, not from headline tone. The item reaches the email labelled as inference, path visible: “Why am I seeing this? MSFT is a major AVGO customer.”

One situation, not five alerts

An earnings release produces an EPS surprise, a revenue surprise, new guidance, a price reaction, and unusual volume. Legacy alerting sends five notifications. Oreum aggregates them into one Intelligence Item — one situation, one explanation, one attention score, evidence attached.

System Architecture

A shared intelligence layer, a private personal layer

Global facts, observations, signals, context, and intelligence are processed once and shared by everyone. Watchlists and positions live in a separate personalization layer, protected by row-level security, and enter the pipeline only at relevance.

Boundaries the system enforces

BoundaryRule
TrustSource data is external and untrusted; normalization validates its shape. LLM extraction output crosses the same boundary and is validated the same way.
ClockLive state and finalized history are different data layers with different freshness semantics; neither impersonates the other.
UserPer-user data is scoped by database-enforced ownership; global reference data is readable by all, writable by none of the client-facing keys.
InferenceObserved facts, known events, and inferred propagation remain distinguishable in storage, in scoring, and on screen.
CacheEvery external data call lands behind a cache or persistence boundary; nothing paid is fetched during rendering.

Foundation in place today

  • Curated company universe and per-user watchlists, RLS-protected
  • Finalized daily OHLCV with gap-filling ingestion and market-calendar tracking
  • Provider payloads schema-validated — a drifted field fails loudly, never silently
  • Cache freshness monitored end-to-end via the health endpoint
  • Authentication (magic link, GitHub, Google) with defense in depth down to the database
  • Feed and Ticker interface shells; intelligence currently exercised by clearly-quarantined fixtures

The conceptual data layers — facts → observations → events → signals → impacts → intelligence items — guide schema boundaries as they materialize; the schema is deliberately not forced to mirror the model prematurely. Because the universe is small and the granularity daily, every derived layer can be recomputed from scratch in one pass — no streaming infrastructure, no incremental-update machinery, full reproducibility by construction.

What Oreum Is Not

Boundaries that will not move

Not a trading platformNo execution, no order flow, no broker features.
Not a stock pickerNo buy/sell calls, no targets, no “top picks.”
Not an investment advisorContext and evidence, never recommendations.
Not a news aggregatorStories are inputs to evaluation, not the product.
Not an AI summarizerThe LLM explains what math decided; it decides nothing.
Not an engagement machineSuccess is measured in attention saved, not sessions.

Oreum never forecasts prices. No targets, no scenarios about where a stock goes, no probability of a move. Technical indicators appear only as context. The universe stays fixed and hand-curated — widening it is a deliberate roadmap decision, never a side effect of a filter.

The product is the restraint. Every system in this brief — the vocabulary that refuses to conflate, the thresholds that permit silence, the graph that proposes but never decides, the model that explains but never scores — exists so that when Oreum speaks, it has earned it. Current Open Questions

Genuinely unresolved — and labelled that way

Everything else in this brief is decided direction. These items are not, and pretending otherwise would break the product's own rule about presenting inference as certainty.

Attention weighting and calibration

The composition of signal strength, context, impact, relevance, and confidence into a score is structurally decided; the weights, threshold levels, and band anchors (“what does an 8 mean?”) are not. They will be set by historical replay against the labeled evaluation set — not declared in advance.

Live-data provider strategy

True real-time vs. 15-minute delayed, which provider, at what cost and rate limits. The decision gates the live phase and will be recorded before integration. The current provider also publishes finalized daily data at an undocumented hour, which determines whether the daily email is a pre-open or a mid-morning product.

Email cadence and timing

Morning (“yesterday's session plus today's calendar”) vs. evening (“today's session”), and whether high-attention situations ever justify a second, event-driven send.

Consensus-estimates source

“Expected vs. actual” facts require licensed estimates data — among the most expensive market-data categories. Until a source is justified, the price reaction plus the classified event carries the story.

News deduplication method

One story from five outlets must become one event with five source links. The starting method is deliberately simple (ticker, title similarity, time window); whether it needs embeddings will be decided against real data, not in the abstract.
